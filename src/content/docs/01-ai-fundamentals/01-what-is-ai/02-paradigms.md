## From rules to learning systems
Early AI systems relied on expert rules and logic engines. These systems were predictable but brittle, because every new edge case required manual updates.

## Statistical learning
Machine learning shifted the paradigm by learning patterns from data. Instead of encoding rules, engineers define objectives and let models infer the mapping. This enabled better generalization, but introduced uncertainty and the need for evaluation discipline.

## Representation learning
Deep learning further reduced feature engineering by learning representations directly from raw data. This improved performance on complex tasks, but increased compute costs and made systems harder to interpret.

## Generative systems
Foundation models and generative AI changed the output space from fixed labels to open-ended sequences. This unlocks flexible interfaces, but demands guardrails, prompting strategy, and safety evaluation.

## Systems takeaway
Each paradigm increases capability and complexity. Production teams must pair the model class with the right data pipelines, testing strategy, and operational controls.
